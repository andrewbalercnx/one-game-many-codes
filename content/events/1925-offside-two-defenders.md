One line in the law book changed how football was played. Here is the whole story in
four beats.

## Before

Under the old law, an attacker needed three opponents — usually two full-backs and the
goalkeeper — nearer their own goal-line than he was. Defenders learned to push up in
unison and catch attackers offside on purpose. The offside trap strangled the game:
fewer chances, fewer goals, a stalemate dressed up as tactics.

## The change

In 1925 the number three became two. That was the entire amendment — one word in one law.

## What happened next

Goals in the Football League jumped by roughly a third in a single season. Nothing about
the players had changed; the constraint had. And as attackers exploited the new space, a
defensive answer was invented to close it: Herbert Chapman's WM formation, which pulled a
player back to guard the gap the old trap used to cover. A rule change had triggered an
arms race.

## What it demonstrates

One line in the law book; a formation revolution. A small change to a constraint produced
a large, permanent change in how the whole game was played and shaped — the site's whole
thesis, delivered inside a single season.
