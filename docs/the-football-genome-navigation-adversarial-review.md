# Navigation model – adversarial review

Method note, stated plainly: these are four adversarial passes I ran in sequence against the first draft of the navigation description, each with a single hostile mandate and instructions to find the strongest possible objections – not four independent autonomous agents. Each challenge below records a verdict: **upheld** (the navigation model was changed), **partial** (changed in part), or **rejected** (with the reason). The published navigation model is the post-review version; this report shows what the first draft claimed and what died.

The first draft, for reference, differed from the published version in these ways: the Map was the primary entry point; lens labels ("gameplay" / "fan") appeared as section chrome; prompts were drawn from the brief's four generic templates; there was no anchor code, no trail, no audio syndication, and no stated treatment of deep entry.

---

## Pass 1 – Usability

*Mandate: assume a first-time visitor of average patience. Find every place they get lost, stuck, or overwhelmed.*

**U1. "The Map as entry point fails the visitor who arrived with a question."** A thirty-node interactive genealogy is a browsing instrument. Most first visits are question-shaped ("why does rugby have scrums"), and forcing them through a taxonomy before an answer is the classic interactive-museum failure: beautiful, admired, abandoned. **Upheld.** The homepage now leads with a rotating question; the Map became a hub one tap away, never forced (§3, §4).

**U2. "Lateral navigation with no hierarchy means no way back."** Question-chains hop across codes; a visitor four hops in has no breadcrumb because there is no tree above them, and the browser back button reconstructs sequence without meaning. **Upheld.** The trail (§2) – the visitor's own question-path, retraceable – exists because of this challenge.

**U3. "Three overlapping metadata systems is badge soup."** The draft surfaced confidence markers (established/probable/contested), evidence tiers, and lens labels as visible chrome. A reader would need a legend to read a page. **Upheld**, jointly with S2 below. One metadata system survives in the chrome; the rest became prose register and authoring discipline (§1).

**U4. "The graph view will frighten the horses."** Multiple ancestry, dashed edges and confidence markers shown to a first-timer reads as an error state. **Upheld.** Guided tree is the default; the graph is an explicit expert switch (§4).

**U5. "The per-page mode switch isn't a podcast."** People who want lean-back want a feed, a series, an order – not a play button scattered across pages. **Upheld.** The episode hub as a proper series surface with cadence (§7).

---

## Pass 2 – Simplicity

*Mandate: count the concepts a visitor must learn. Kill every one that isn't pulling its weight.*

**S1. "The site has more organising concepts than a mid-size museum."** Five layers, three cross-cutting features, two lenses, two modes, three tiers, three confidence levels. The visitor wanted to know why American football keeps stopping. **Upheld** as the review's most important finding. The principle "the taxonomy is invisible" (§1) is its direct product: all of it stays as authoring machinery; none of it appears as interface vocabulary.

**S2. "'Gameplay lens' and 'fan lens' are editorial concepts leaking into the UI."** The two-lens separation is a discipline for writers, not a label for readers. **Upheld.** The sections are titled *On the pitch* and *In the stands*; the discipline survives untouched underneath.

**S3. "The homepage does too much."** The draft's homepage carried the thesis, the Map, the episode rail, compare, and the timeline. **Upheld.** One question plus three doors; everything else demoted (§3).

**S4. "Kill the trail – it's another concept."** **Rejected.** The trail replaces the breadcrumb rather than adding to it, and U2 established the disorientation problem is real. One concept in, one concept out.

**S5. "Kill the graph view entirely – the tree is enough."** **Rejected.** The graph view is where the editorial policy on contested history lives, and the credibility outcome (brief §10.2) depends on it existing. Demoted, not deleted.

---

## Pass 3 – Completeness

*Mandate: enumerate the journeys the draft never mentions. Every unhandled arrival or exit is a defect.*

**C1. "There is no search."** The draft had none. A site whose glossary is its connective tissue, without search, strands every visitor who knows a word ("maul") but not a question. **Upheld.** Glossary-first search, results phrased as questions (§6).

**C2. "Deep entry is the majority case and the draft ignores it."** Search engines and shared links will deliver most first visits to interior pages. The draft designed only the homepage journey. **Upheld**, and promoted to the model's first principle (§1, §8).

**C3. "No returning-visitor story."** What brings anyone back, and what do they see changed? **Upheld.** Recency-weighted homepage question and episode badging, deliberately without accounts or notifications (§8).

**C4. "Podcast-style but not on podcast platforms is a half-measure."** If the mode is worth building, the audio derivative belongs where people actually listen. **Upheld.** Audio syndication of every episode (§7).

**C5. "Glossary entries and timeline entries are dead ends."** The end-with-a-door rule covered substantial pages only. **Upheld** in reduced form: glossary entries end with their shared-inheritance sibling or citing rule page; timeline entries were already links by design.

**C6. "No offline mode, no saved reading lists, no multi-language plan."** **Rejected** for v1 – real gaps, wrong document. Language strategy in particular is a content-brief-scale decision (the canon's codes have non-English heartlands) and is noted for the brief's next revision rather than smuggled into navigation.

---

## Pass 4 – Engagement

*Mandate: assume the visitor is one tap from leaving at all times. Find what's boring, repetitive, or unrewarding.*

**E1. "Templated prompts become wallpaper by page three."** The brief's four recurring question-types, rendered literally, would train visitors to ignore the site's only navigation. **Upheld.** Prompts are authored specifically per page; the templates survive only as the author's checklist (§1).

**E2. "Neutrality is admirable and flat. Give the visitor a side."** The site refuses to rank codes, which removes the partisanship that fuels sports engagement. **Upheld in transformed form** – the review's best idea. The anchor code (§5): the site stays neutral, the visitor gets a vantage point, and every journey becomes "my game versus the unfamiliar". This also directly serves the brief's primary traversal outcome.

**E3. "All cliffhangers, no payoffs."** If every page ends by raising a question, the visitor is always owed and never paid. **Partial.** The model now treats shared-inheritance reveals (mark/fair catch as fossils of one rule) as designed payoff moments surfaced through the anchor code's glossary behaviour; a fuller "reward beat" per chain is flagged for the content brief rather than the navigation layer.

**E4. "Twelve to fifteen episodes is a thin channel; visitors subscribe to cadence, not slates."** **Partial.** The hub states a cadence (§7) and the brief's outcome 4 (evolution-driven updates) supplies the ongoing material; the honest residual is that cadence is a production commitment no navigation model can conjure.

**E5. "Add progression – badges, completion, 'you've explored 4 of 12 rules'."** **Rejected.** Gamification chrome sits badly on a site whose credibility outcome depends on being taken seriously by historians. The trail already shows distance travelled without scoring it.

---

# What changed, in order of consequence

1. The taxonomy went invisible (S1/S2/U3) – the machinery stays, the vocabulary goes.
2. Deep entry became the primary designed case (C2).
3. The anchor code was added (E2) – neutrality for the site, a vantage point for the visitor.
4. The question-led front door replaced the Map-as-homepage (U1/S3).
5. The trail replaced hierarchy breadcrumbs (U2/S4).
6. The episode hub, cadence and audio syndication replaced the per-page play button (U5/C4).

Two residuals no navigation document can fix, stated per the review's own rules: episode cadence is a production commitment (E4), and the language question is a content-brief decision waiting to be made (C6).
